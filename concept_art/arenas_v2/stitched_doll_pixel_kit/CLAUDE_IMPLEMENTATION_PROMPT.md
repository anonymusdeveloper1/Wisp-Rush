# Claude implementation prompt — Stitchwarden's Vigil

Implement **Stitchwarden's Vigil** as a new, playable, layered Endless arena in `D:\Wisp Rush` using the art kit in this folder. The kit is also copied to a Desktop folder with this same name. Keep the existing Quarry Titan and current painted Stitched Doll Jungle arena playable and visually unchanged. Do not replace their art with this kit. The owner previously chose free access for testing the dark doll arena; make this new version available to equip for that test. If another catalog field needs a design decision that the owner has not made, ask rather than inventing it.

## Owner's target

- This is a **2D pixel-art** arena, not a 3D scene. It should have the depth and strong staging of the dark jungle reference at `reference/scene.png`, assembled from independent sprites.
- The giant stitched doll holds the top of a **warm beige, narrow arena board** in a dark moonlit jungle. Keep the active floor somewhat smaller than the earlier full-height picture. The board has a **mild visual perspective** from its frame/bevel, while all four playable walls stay a straight, axis-aligned rectangle. The doll, scythe and entire board stay visible.
- The doll watches the run with a small head turn. Two long cloth panels make smooth, flexible wind waves. The fire on the scythe visibly burns; it is more than a colour pulse. Lanterns flicker. Wisps drift, low fog moves, and occasional small flying creatures cross the distant background. These are decorative and stay out of the playable floor.
- **Small and large screens use different compositions.** Phones show the complete central doll-and-board group with the portrait jungle. On a larger portrait screen, the arena group grows with usable screen size and more scenery becomes visible at its sides. Do not stretch, crop, or cover up the board to fill a tablet. The wider jungle plate and the separate side layers exist for that purpose. Keep the HUD readable and its safe areas clear.

## Scene and motion

1. Make an arena-specific visual scene or equivalent reusable component selected by this arena's data. Keep legacy image-only arenas on their current path. Mount the layered visual behind enemies, hazards, player, pickups and HUD. Preserve existing save and Shop behavior and create a real thumbnail of the assembled arena.
2. Build one canonical local coordinate space for the **central group**: doll, scythe, cloth attachment points, floor, frame, lanterns and effects. Calculate one uniform scale and translation per viewport. Derive the screen-space floor rectangle from the exact same transform; feed it to all existing player/enemy/hazard/boss geometry. The frame suggests perspective but neither the rendered floor nor its collision rotates or narrows at one end.
3. Fit the whole centre group within the usable portrait view on a small phone, leaving HUD/safe-area clearance. On larger screens, increase its uniform scale as usable space increases until the limiting dimension is reached; expose more side scenery from `jungle_wide.png` and the side sprites. Do not independently scale X/Y. Do not simply cover-scale a single finished portrait image. Recalculate art and collision together on viewport resize.
4. Rig the two cloth panels as deformable 2D meshes (for example, weighted `Polygon2D` with short `Skeleton2D`/`Bone2D` chains or equivalent). Keep their roots fixed and move the remaining points in a smooth traveling wave with delayed tips. Inspect the repo's existing rig recipe and spring code before reusing it. The panels should not flip between a few rigid poses or expose seams.
5. Pivot the separate head subtly toward the active Wisp while the hood hides its joint. Play the four scythe-flame frames as a looping blade-attached overlay; let the flame change shape, with colour/light variation only as a secondary effect. Flicker lantern light without moving the lantern housings.
6. Loop the wisp and flying-creature frames while moving their instances on paths in the **scenery**. Keep creatures farther back than the board. Move the fog slowly behind the frame. Never place decorative sprites, fog or light effects over the playable floor where they can look like enemies, pickups, hazards or attack telegraphs. Pause scenery with the run; respect the existing Reduced Motion setting with restrained or static alternatives.
7. Use nearest texture filtering and the project's pixel-art rules. Avoid blur, gradient, painterly effects and subpixel crawling. Keep animation and draw cost reasonable on phones.
